# Reading the character

The heart of kawaii design is understanding who the character is. "Cute" for a small child-like character and "cute" for a grown-up character are different things. The same pastel page can be right for one and embarrassing for the other.

Read the character first, then let it decide the type, shapes, color, copy and motion. When there is no character, read the product's persona in the same way.

## What to read

Collect this from the art, the official profile, the character's own words and the existing site. Write it down in the direction as the **Character** line.

| Axis | Read from | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Apparent age** | Proportions (head-to-body ratio, eye size), outfit, self-introduction, stated age | 幼い (child-like, chibi), 少女 (teen), 大人 (grown-up) |
| **Temperament** | Expression, pose, how they talk | 元気, おっとり, 眠たげ, クール, ダウナー, 照れ屋, 上品, いたずら好き |
| **Voice** | Their own text: first person, sentence endings, kana ratio, symbols | 「〜だよ！」, 「〜です。」, 「…ねむい」, many ひらがな, few exclamation marks |
| **Signature details** | Accessories, colors, motifs on the character | cat ears, a moon hairpin, a bell, ribbons, a frilled apron |

Design for the character's fans. Don't tone the character down for a general audience. Everything, including headings, labels and decoration, follows the character.

## From character to design

### Apparent age

| | 幼い | 少女 | 大人 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Text** | Mostly ひらがな in headings and labels (「じこしょうかい」「すきなもの」). Short words. Kanji only where hiragana would be hard to read, or with `<ruby>` | Mixed kana and kanji, a few playful words | Normal kanji usage. Hiragana only as a deliberate accent |
| **Display type** | Very round and chunky: Mochiy Pop One, M PLUS Rounded 1c ExtraBold, Hachi Maru Pop | Rounded or pop: Zen Maru Gothic Bold, Kiwi Maru, Yusei Magic | Thin and light: a light Latin sans at regular or light weight (Jost, Josefin Sans, Montserrat Light, Inter Light), Zen Kaku Gothic Antique Light, Zen Old Mincho, Shippori Mincho |
| **Size and weight** | Large, bold, generous line height | Medium-large | A big but thin title (weight 300 to 400), and small body text: 12 to 14px with a line height of about 2 and slight letter spacing |
| **Shapes** | Circles and very round corners, chunky outlines if the art has them, toy-like buttons | Rounded rectangles plus circles, stickers, bubbles | Smaller radius (6 to 14px), thin lines, more straight edges, one curved detail |
| **Color** | Clear, bright pastels or candy colors, high key | Pastels with an accent, ゆめかわ | 透明感 or dusty tones (くすみカラー), fewer colors, more white |
| **Decoration** | Toys, stationery, stickers, faces on things, hand-drawn doodles | Sparkles, bubbles, ribbons as accents | One refined detail: a ribbon, a thin line drawing, a small motif |
| **Space** | Big targets, simple layout, fewer items per screen | Normal | More whitespace, quieter rhythm, an editorial feel |
| **Motion** | Bouncy, a little hop on everything they touch | Floaty, sparkly arrivals | Slow fades and gentle rises. No bounce |

### Temperament

| Temperament | Color | Shape and layout | Copy | Motion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **元気** | Saturated, warm accent | Tilted stickers, bold blocks | Exclamations allowed in the character's own voice | Quick, bouncy |
| **おっとり / 眠たげ** | Soft, low contrast, warm or milky | Very round, lots of space, things leaning or resting | Slow, trailing 「〜」 | Slow floating, blinking, a long sway |
| **クール / ダウナー** | Cooler hues, near-monochrome plus one accent | Less round, more straight lines, asymmetric | Short and dry, few exclamation marks | Minimal, precise, no bounce |
| **照れ屋** | Soft blush tones | Things peeking from edges, half-hidden | Short, a little shy | Peeking in, small hops |
| **上品** | Muted or 透明感, gold or deep accent | Thin lines, symmetry, serif titles | Polite です・ます | Slow fades |
| **いたずら好き** | Bright accent, a surprising color | Off-kilter, overlapping | Playful | Little surprises on hover |

Characters usually mix two temperaments (眠たげ + 照れ屋, クール + 元気). Pick the main one for the structure and the second one for small details.

### Voice

- Write UI labels and headings in the character's voice: if they say 「すきなもの」, don't title the section 「好きなもの」 or "Favorites".
- Match the kana ratio of their own text. A character who writes in hiragana gets hiragana labels. A character who writes normally gets normal kanji.
- Quote their own words exactly. Don't invent catchphrases for a real character.

## Combining age with a dialect

Apparent age is a layer on top of the dialect, not a dialect of its own. A grown-up character can still live in a ゆめかわ world: keep the dialect's background and world (the iridescent gradient, glass, bubbles), and let the age decide the type, text size, radius and motion (thin type, small body text, small radius, slow fades). The same works the other way: a child-like character in a 大人かわいい palette keeps the round, hiragana type.

## 大人かわいい in more detail

Grown-up cuteness looks very different from the other ages. It is cute held back, with thin type, small text and one point of sweetness.

- **Type**: thin. A large title in a light or regular Latin sans or a refined mincho, never bold and never chunky. Body text is small (12 to 14px) with a line height around 2 and a little letter spacing, so the page feels airy and editorial.
- **Color**: one flat pale background (lemon, ivory, pale pink, pale blue) and one accent color for the title and links (coral, rose, deep blue). Few colors in total.
- **Structure**: lots of whitespace, few elements per screen, an editorial grid.
- **Shape**: small radius or none, hairline rules, straight edges, with one soft shape such as an arch-cropped photo or an oval frame.
- **The sweet point**: exactly one per screen. An arch frame, a small ribbon, a hand-drawn line, a soft pink accent.
- **Copy**: polite and short.
- **Motion**: slow fades and small rises. Nothing bounces.
- **Pitfall**: too quiet becomes generic minimal. If nobody would call it cute, warm up the background color or bring back the sweet point.

## Checklist

- Can you describe the character in one line: apparent age, main temperament, second temperament, voice?
- Do the type, shapes, color and motion match that line? Would the character feel at home on this page?
- Are headings and labels written in the character's voice and kana ratio?
- Would a fan of this character feel the page was made for them?
